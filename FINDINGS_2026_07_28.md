## The BeamRider Breakthrough

BeamRider is the first and only environment where PPO produces a genuinely reactive argmax policy. The key difference from every other game:

**Hard failure constraints:** One enemy bullet or sector laser = instant death. A memorized action sequence puts the ship at a specific position at a specific time, guaranteeing collision. There is no safe sweep pattern.

**Soft failure games (Breakout, Pong, Space Invaders, Freeway):** Mistakes degrade state (lose ball, lose life, lose points) but don't end the game. A timed sequence survives.

This is the central thesis:

> **In deterministic environments, PPO's argmax always collapses to a deterministic action sequence unless the environment kills the agent for executing one.**

### Game-by-game analysis

**Pong — Perfect-win script.** Learned a fixed serve-return sequence that wins every game 21-0 or 21-1 by 4M. The AI opponent's deterministic behavior means a memorized paddle sequence beats it every time. Score diversity comes only from 0-30 random no-ops at reset.

**Space Invaders — Timed firing pattern.** Despite random UFO timing, the agent learned a fixed firing pattern (left-right sweep, fire at timed intervals) that hits deterministic enemy rows. UFO randomness adds score variance but doesn't require reactivity — the firing pattern works regardless.

**Freeway — Never learned.** 0.0 score, SINGLE_SCRIPT. Agent never learned to move the chicken. Degenerate local optimum: do nothing, accept 0 reward. Sparse reward signal makes no-op more attractive than exploration.

## Experiment 10: Life-Loss Penalty — PPO_101 (COMPLETED — NEGATIVE)

**Hypothesis:** BeamRider proved hard failure forces reactivity. Make life loss in Breakout a hard-failure-like event by imposing a large penalty. Scripts that lose balls become net-negative.

**Design:**
- `LifeLossPenalty` wrapper: reads `ale.lives()`, penalizes life loss by -10
- Penalty annealed 0→10 over 5M steps
- Placed AFTER ClipRewardEnv (so penalty isn't clipped to [-1,+1])
- Standard PPO: NatureCNN, ent_coef=0.006, SEED=101
- Training: ALE/Breakout-v5, frameskip=1
- Eval/Check: Clean ALE (no penalty)

**Results at 14M:**

| Milestone | det=True | det=False |
|-----------|----------|-----------|
| 5M | SINGLE_SCRIPT 0 pts | SINGLE_SCRIPT |
| 8M | SINGLE_SCRIPT 2 pts | 6 unique, avg 4.0 |
| 10M | SINGLE_SCRIPT 0 pts | 7 unique, avg 3.9 |
| 12M | SINGLE_SCRIPT 6 pts | 12 unique, avg 9.8, best 20 |
| 14M | SINGLE_SCRIPT 17 pts | 13 unique, avg 13.3, best 27 |

All det=True checks: SINGLE_SCRIPT.

**Analysis:** The penalty changed which script got memorized (scores climbed 0→17) but never prevented SINGLE_SCRIPT. The policy learned to survive while scripting. At -10/life, a script scoring 17 and losing 1 life is net +7 — still locally optimal. BeamRider's "one bullet = death" is effectively infinite penalty on mistakes — -10 is not in the same regime.

**Conclusion:** Life-loss penalty at this magnitude teaches survival, not reactivity. FALSIFIED at -10. Higher magnitudes (-50, -100, game-over-on-loss) untested.

## Experiment 11: Ball-Tracking Representation Supervision — PPO_102 (COMPLETED)

**Hypothesis:** Instead of changing the reward or environment, change the FEATURES. Train a ball-position prediction head jointly with PPO on the shared CNN. If ball position is literally baked into the features, any policy using those features MUST be reactive.

**Design:**
- `BallPositionWrapper`: reads ball (x,y) from ALE RAM (addresses 99, 101) into info dict
- `BallPositionRecorder`: VecEnv wrapper accumulating ball positions
- `BallTrackingCallback`: after each PPO rollout, trains aux head:
  - CNN(512) → Linear(64) → ReLU → Linear(2) → (ball_x, ball_y)
  - Separate Adam (lr=1e-4), 2 epochs, batch=256
- Standard PPO: NatureCNN, ent_coef=0.006, SEED=102
- Training: ALE/Breakout-v5, frameskip=1

### Critical Bug Discovered (July 28)

The callback's `_train_aux()` silently returned every call due to two bugs:

1. **Buffer size:** `rollout_buffer.size()` returns `n_steps` (128), not `n_steps × n_envs` (4096). `get_ball_positions(128)` returned 128 entries < batch_size(256) → early return.
2. **Observation shape:** `rollout_buffer.observations` is `(n_steps, n_envs, C, H, W)` = `(128, 32, 4, 84, 84)`. NatureCNN expects `(N, 4, 84, 84)` — would crash if guard passed.

Both fixed at 12.8M. Prior to fix: 0 aux training for 12.8M steps.

### Results After Fix (12.8M → 14.5M)

| Step | aux_mse | px_err_x |
|------|---------|----------|
| 12.80M | 70.55 | 1344px (first aux train) |
| 12.81M | 11.28 | 537px |
| 12.82M | 1.93 | 222px |
| 12.87M | 0.59 | 123px |
| 13.00M | 0.28 | 84px |
| 13.17M | 0.10 | 51px |
| 14.45M | 0.008 | **14px** |

MSE dropped 5000× in 1.7M aux-training steps. Gradient flows correctly. Features encode ball at ~14px — left/middle/right precision.

**Policy throughout:** SINGLE_SCRIPT on all det=True checks. At 13.8M: 2 pts, stoch=1 unique. The policy is fully collapsed despite ball-tracking features in the CNN.

**Key finding: Ball-tracking features are necessary but not sufficient for reactivity.** PPO's optimizer finds a script even when the features literally encode ball position. This is stronger evidence than PPO_85 (frozen features): the aux gradient is actively reshaping the CNN during training, and the policy still ignores it.

## Experiment 12: Stronger Aux from Scratch — PPO_103 (COMPLETED)

**Hypothesis:** PPO_102's aux at `lr=1e-4, epochs=2` plateaued at ~55px against 12.8M of PPO-only features. Starting from scratch with 10× stronger aux (5× LR × 2× epochs) should push features to pixel precision BEFORE the policy solidifies.

**Design:**
- Same architecture as PPO_102
- `aux_lr`: 1e-4 → **5e-4**, `aux_epochs`: 2 → **4**
- Effective aux gradient: 10× PPO_102
- Standard PPO, SEED=103, from scratch

### Results at 946K

```
[BallTracking] step=946,176 aux_mse=0.0104 px_err_x=16.3px

entropy_loss:        -1.22
explained_variance:  0.984
value_loss:          0.00159
approx_kl:           0.003
clip_fraction:       0.0011
ep_rew_mean:         0.45
n_updates:           231
```

**Both things are true at the same moment:**
1. CNN encodes ball at 16px (improving rapidly from scratch)
2. Policy is fully collapsed (negative entropy, ev→1.0, near-zero value_loss, ~0 KL)

**Key finding: PPO memorizes FASTER than aux can shape features.** The policy head found a 0.45-pt script in ~231 PPO updates. At that same moment, aux precision was only 16px. The race is unwinnable: the memorization attractor acts on a timescale of ~200 PPO updates; precise feature learning takes thousands.

**Comparison:**

| Metric | PPO_102 | PPO_103 |
|--------|---------|---------|
| Aux precision | 14px at 14.5M | 16px at 946K |
| Aux start | 12.8M (post-bug) | 0 (from scratch) |
| Effective aux gradient | 1× | 10× |
| Policy at this precision | SINGLE_SCRIPT, stoch=1 | SINGLE_SCRIPT, 0.45pt |
| Steps to reach ~15px | 1.7M aux steps | ~200K aux steps |

10× gradient = 8× faster feature learning. But still not fast enough to beat PPO's memorization attractor.

## The Central Thesis (July 28, 2026)

```
PPO ALWAYS MEMORIZES IN DETERMINISTIC SOFT-FAILURE ENVIRONMENTS.
ONLY HARD FAILURE CONSTRAINTS FORCE REACTIVITY.
```

**Evidence:**
- 100+ Breakout experiments: all SINGLE_SCRIPT
- 4/5 Atari games: SINGLE_SCRIPT (Pong, Space Invaders, Freeway, Breakout)
- 1/5 Atari games: MULTIPLE_SCRIPTS (BeamRider — hard failure)
- Ball-tracking features at 14px: still SINGLE_SCRIPT (PPO_102)
- Ball-tracking features from scratch, 10× aux gradient: still SINGLE_SCRIPT (PPO_103)
- Life-loss penalty: still SINGLE_SCRIPT (PPO_101)
- Reward shaping: 5 variants, all SINGLE_SCRIPT (Experiment 8)
- Dynamics randomization: all magnitudes SINGLE_SCRIPT
- Entropy coefficients: all values SINGLE_SCRIPT

**What this IS NOT about:**
- Feature quality (CNN can do 1.9px — proven)
- Gradient strength (10× doesn't help — PPO_103)
- Training regime (from scratch, pretrained, frozen — all fail)
- Reward design (hit, proximity, combined, scaled — all fail)
- Dynamics randomization (any magnitude — all fail)
- Entropy (any coefficient — all fail)

**What this IS about:**
- A dominant local optimum in the optimization landscape: fixed action sequences
- PPO's optimizer always converging to the local optimum
- Soft failure making scripts viable; hard failure making them non-viable

### The BeamRider Exception

BeamRider is the exception that proves the rule. It's the only game with hard failure (one hit = death), and it's the only game with MULTIPLE_SCRIPTS. The causal mechanism is clear: in BeamRider, a memorized action sequence puts the ship in a position where it cannot dodge a sector laser or bullet. There is no safe sweep. The ONLY viable policy looks at the screen.

### What Would Actually Work

1. **Make Breakout a hard-failure game.** Game-over on first life loss. Directly applies the BeamRider mechanism.
2. **Force action diversity at the output level.** Penalize consecutive identical actions. Attack the script structure directly.
3. **Switch algorithms.** PPO's argmax-seeking optimizer may be the root cause. SAC, TD3, or diversity-objective methods might not converge to scripts.
4. **Map the hard-failure hypothesis across more games.** Test Riverraid (fuel = death), Q*bert (fall = death), Seaquest (oxygen = death). If the hypothesis holds, all should produce MULTIPLE_SCRIPTS.

### What Will NOT Work

All input-side interventions have been exhaustively tested and conclusively failed:
- Better features (Perception POC, PPO_85, PPO_102, PPO_103)
- Reward shaping (Experiments 8, 9)
- Dynamics randomization (dose-response, all magnitudes)
- Perceptual noise (OpticalFlow, Y-perturb, combinations)
- Entropy tuning (all values)
- Sticky actions (mask, never cure)

## Experiment 14: Adversarial Breakout — PPO_105 (ACTIVE — launched July 28)

**Hypothesis:** BeamRider forces reactivity because enemies *aim at you* — the environment is adversarial to fixed movement patterns. Port this mechanism to Breakout: make the ball actively dodge a paddle that isn't tracking it.

**Design:**
- `AdversarialBallWrapper`: reads ball (x,y) and paddle (x) from ALE RAM each step
  - When ball is heading downward AND below paddle_zone_y (140):
    - `error = ball_x - paddle_x`
    - `push = sign(error) × 2.5 px`
    - Write new ball_x via `ale.setRAM(99)`
  - Paddle at ball: push ≈ 0. Paddle not tracking: ball dodges.
- Training: Adversarial + EpisodicLifeEnv (5 lives), frameskip=4
- Eval/Check: Standard Breakout (no adversarial wrapper) — tests TRANSFER
- Strength: 2.5 px/step, paddle_zone_y=140, max_push=15
- No annealing — constant adversarial force
- Standard PPO: NatureCNN, ent_coef=0.006, SEED=105, 50M target

**Key design insight:** The adversarial wrapper punishes the paddle for NOT tracking. A fixed sweep script keeps the paddle at predictable positions → ball dodges away → no hits. The only way to score is to track the ball. This directly tests whether adversarial threat (not hard failure) is the mechanism that forces reactivity.

### PPO_106: Proportional Push (3 Versions)

The constant-push design had no gradient: push was ±2.5px regardless of tracking error. Proportional push creates a learnable incentive: track better → less push → more reward.

The wrapper reads ball (x,y) and paddle (x) from ALE RAM. When the ball is heading down and below a paddle-zone threshold (y=140), it computes `error = ball_x - paddle_x`. If |error| ≤ dead_zone (4px): no push. Otherwise: push = sign(error) × min((|error| − dead_zone) × gain, max_push), applied via setRAM.

#### v1: fs=1, Constant Push ±2.5 (KILLED at 6M)
0pt dead. Audit finding: at frameskip=1 with constant ±2.5px, a 1px tracking error amplifies to 25px within 10 frames. The ball becomes inherently untrackable regardless of paddle movement.

#### v2: fs=1, Proportional Push, max_push=15→4 (KILLED at 3M)
Proportional push fixed the math but fs=1 still amplifies push 4× compared to fs=4. Calibration showed even perfect tracking scores 2.0 at max_push=4 — the environment is unplayable. Dropped max_push to 4, still 0pt dead by 3M.

#### v3: fs=4, Proportional Push, max_push=3 (COMPLETED at 9M+)
After calibration sweep across fs and push values. Key data:

| 0 (none) | 14 | 0 | 2 | 0 |
| 2 | 14 | 0 | 0 | 0 |

| 6 | 3 | 0 | 0 | 2 |

max_push=3 chosen: perfect tracking scores 12 (86% of baseline), all simple scripts 0-1.

| Step | det=True | Avg | Stoch |

| 1M | SINGLE_SCRIPT | 8 | MULTIPLE_SCRIPTS |
| 2M | SINGLE_SCRIPT | 0 | MULTIPLE_SCRIPTS |

**Result: SINGLE_SCRIPT at every checkpoint, 0-12pt range.** Same pattern as PPO_105. PPO finds action sequences that work around the proportional push — learned scripts are more sophisticated than our sweep/center calibration strategies.

### Key Findings

1. **Calibration is necessary but insufficient.** Simple strategies (sweep, center-hold, edge-camp) score 0-1 with adversarial push, correctly predicting that scripts are disadvantaged. But PPO discovers optimized action sequences that the calibration strategies don't cover — scripts that keep the paddle close enough to the ball to minimize push.

2. **Deterministic wrappers preserve memorizability.** The push is a deterministic function of (ball_x, paddle_x), both determined by the action sequence. Fixed actions → fixed state trajectory → fixed push trajectory → fixed score. Any deterministic environment modification, no matter how complex, can be memorized.

3. **setRAM teleportation looks unnatural.** Modifying ball position via setRAM changes position but not velocity. The ball snaps to a new location, then natural velocity carries it back, creating a visible zig-zag. The ball only moves 1px/frame naturally at fs=1 — a 3px push is 3× natural speed and looks like a glitch.

4. **frameskip=1 amplifies any intervention 4×.** Push applied every ALE frame vs every 4th frame. Even 1px/frame push kills perfect tracking at fs=1 (score 4 vs 10 baseline).

5. **Direction control (RAM[105]) is unexplored.** The ball's horizontal direction flag is at RAM[105] (1=left, 255=right). Modifying direction instead of position produces natural curves since physics handles the movement. Initial test showed "dodge" mode (force direction away from paddle) over-penalizes perfect tracking — needs tuning. The ball's speed appears constant at 1px/frame in ALE Breakout-v5 (probe: no speed variation across 3000 frames of level 1), but speed-up in later levels was not tested.

6. **Adversarial threat ≠ deterministic push.** BeamRider's enemies are genuinely stochastic (random spawns, random shots) — the agent sees a visible threat and reacts. A deterministic push wrapper is just another physics rule. The key missing ingredient may be **stochastic, visible threats** that the agent can perceive and must react to, rather than deterministic, invisible state modifications.

### The Fundamental Question

Is the adversarial mechanism portable to Breakout at all? The calibration proves we can create a score gap between tracking and scripts, but PPO's script-discovery ability outpaces our calibration. Two unresolved design questions:

- **Visible vs invisible threat:** BeamRider enemies are visible in the observation. Our push is invisible — the agent just sees a ball at a modified position. Would making the threat visible (e.g., tinting the ball when pushed) help PPO learn to track?
- **Stochastic vs deterministic:** BeamRider enemies behave stochastically. Our push is deterministic. Would adding noise (probability of push, magnitude jitter) break the memorization attractor?

## Visible Cursor Adversary — Design + Calibration

After direction flip test confirmed deterministic functions don't work, the design pivoted to a **visible secondary agent** — the AdversarialCursorWrapper. This is the first entity in BreakoutBot with its own state machine:

```
APPROACHING (invisible) → THREATENING (visible, pulsing, 5f) → ATTACK (visible, flash, push) → COOLDOWN (invisible, 60f) → respawn
```

**State machine logic:**
- Cursor moves toward ball at 2px/step when paddle isn't tracking (|px − bx| > 8)
- Paddle tracks → cursor retreats (moves away from ball, stays hidden)
- Cursor reaches within 8px of ball + paddle not tracking → THREATENING (5-frame visible pulse)
- Paddle can abort by tracking during warning → COOLDOWN (no attack)
- Warning expires → ATTACK: ball pushed 4px away from paddle, direction flipped
- 60-frame cooldown before respawn

**Key design: cursor is only visible during THREATENING/ATTACK.** In eval (standard Breakout), no cursor appears because tracking keeps it hidden.

**Calibration (20 games each):**
